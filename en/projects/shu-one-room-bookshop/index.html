Operations Management Lead

Yijian Bookshop: making daily operations sustainable

Shanghai University student reading space · Operations Management Lead

As Operations Management Lead from 2022 to 2023, I worked on reopening logistics, book-shelf mapping and cataloguing preparation, meeting records, purchasing and cross-team event support. I also wrote an operating proposal for two postcard collections.

Jun 2022 - Jun 2023 Operations · Process design · Records management · Event support · Cross-team coordination

A campus space that had to work every day

Yijian Bookshop is a student-run reading and gathering space at Shanghai University. Started by students in the School of Liberal Arts in 2009, it moved to the South Campus in 2014. Around my term, the space went through renovation and a reopening process. Alongside reading and study, it has hosted book discussions, film nights, book exchanges and World Book Day activities.

I served as Operations Management Lead from June 2022 to June 2023. Operations work is less visible than a single event: whether the room can open, whether supplies are in place and whether a meeting decision has a clear owner depend on routines. My focus was to make those routines explicit, keep records and coordinate with the other teams.

Daily operations and closing safely

Meeting notes from autumn 2022 show the team revisiting how the space was used and marked as open. We discussed a reversible sign showing the opening hours or that the room was closed. After earlier unauthorized entries, evening-shift members were reminded to check the windows and doors before leaving. A safety reminder only works when it becomes part of the closing routine.

During renovation, operations also followed practical needs such as locks, furniture, lighting and everyday supplies. We purchased items as needed, kept supporting documents and coordinated reimbursement with the bookshop lead. As the room returned to use, the team also had to prepare supplies and spaces for activities: “reopening” was a sequence of small tasks rather than a single announcement.

Recording who did what and when

The 2022–2023 operations summary I maintained used task, date and participating members as its basic record. It covered more than headline events: recruitment and member information, a new shift rota, meeting minutes, window-lock checks and installation, warning signs, electrical and light-strip repairs, buying and installing tables, chairs and bulbs, World Book Day gifts, and supplies for the reopening ceremony. Small tasks such as moving shelves and checking furnishings could also be traced through the records.

Keeping this work log made individual responsibilities visible, helped the team follow progress and gave the next lead context for follow-up purchases and handover. Without it, many small but necessary tasks would be buried in chat histories, with no clear record of why they were needed or how far they had progressed.

Book management: turning shelves into assignable work

Book cataloguing was a piece of work I wanted the next team to continue. The archive contains before-and-after shelf photographs, an earlier inventory spreadsheet and a shelf-mapping plan. The operations summary records book classification and exporting the earlier catalogue; the next step was to use the photographs to break the room into numbered shelf areas, then assign those areas with a lead and deputy coordinators. This turned “catalogue every book” from one vague task into smaller areas that could be claimed and reported on.

Before entering titles, the collection needed sorting: older books, magazines, reference books and comics had to be reviewed separately to decide what to keep, organize or remove. Cataloguing could then proceed in batches by shelf area. The renovation meant the shelves might change, so the full inventory was postponed until after the work. At handover, I left the shelf maps, inventory materials and allocation plan, and marked full cataloguing as still outstanding rather than describing preparation as a completed inventory.

Meeting records and handover

Operations also meant recording decisions so that they could return to daily work. The October 2022 meeting covered opening signs, closing checks, editorial review of posts, bookshop merchandise and film nights. In February 2023, the team discussed cleaning after renovation, locks and soft furnishings, reopening communications and an opening ceremony. Notes grouped follow-up tasks by team, so publicity, HR, event planning and operations could each see what to take forward.

Around the leadership transition, I organized operating notes and open items covering meeting records, supplies, book-shelf areas, purchasing documents and follow-up. The next lead would not have to guess what had happened or which tasks remained. This kind of handover matters because a campus space lasts longer than any one student committee.

From World Book Day to a postcard proposal

For World Book Day in spring 2022, the bookshop collected reading lists and audio readings online. Recommendations ranged from fiction, mystery and history to subject reading and personal reflections. Recordings added each reader’s voice and interpretation to the shared list. I supported the operations side and event supplies, helping the bookshop stay connected with readers when in-person activities were limited.

Later that year, I wrote a postcard proposal as another way to carry the bookshop’s content beyond its shelves. It planned two collections—one based on the twenty-four solar terms and one on Shanghai University scenes—with set sizes, estimated production costs, suggested prices, online pre-orders, collection steps and responsibilities across teams. The proposal records a plan for discussion; its cost estimates are not sales results. Read the full breakdown in the postcard proposal article.

Looking back

Operations was more than “miscellaneous tasks.” It meant connecting the room, safety, supplies, books, activities and team handover into routines that could keep working. Renovation interrupts routines and a leadership change takes away familiar knowledge; maps, records and clear handovers give the next team a place to start.
