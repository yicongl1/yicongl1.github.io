What kind of podcast is it

“The Podcast of Engineers” was started by engineering students and enthusiasts from UTSEUS (Université de Technologie Sino-Européenne de Shanghai, Shanghai University), with five coordinators splitting the work of topic selection, guest outreach, recording and publishing. The show interviews engineers, former engineers, founders, managers and professors in Chinese, French and English — not about the “responsibilities” in a job description, but about what concrete people actually run into in concrete work.

We believe in one sentence: engineering without humanity is devoid of soul. So conversations often start from robotics, aerospace, civil engineering or computer science and wander into literature, philosophy, history, language, art, ecology and psychology.

My role

As one of the five coordinators I worked on topic planning, guest outreach and the production flow, and hosted some of the episodes. Cross-language production is also something I care about: the same show has to stay listenable and information-dense in three linguistic contexts.

Production flow

  • Topics: find the intersection between the guest’s work and what listeners actually wonder about
  • Outline: agree with the guest on the boundaries of the conversation beforehand, avoiding vague “success story” questions
  • Recording: mostly remote, 60–90 minutes per episode
  • Publishing: Bilibili as the main platform, mirrored on the website (Hugo + GitHub Pages), episode metadata managed in Markdown
  • Visuals: a consistent cover system across all 14 episodes

Guests

The 14 guests span two engineering education systems and two career paths: data and engineering roles at Meituan, Microsoft, Google, McDonald’s and Equativ on one side; professors and a former president from École Centrale de Lyon, Paris-Saclay, Kaiserslautern-Landau and CUHK on the other — plus an SDN pioneer and an AI founder.
