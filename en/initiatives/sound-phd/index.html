PhD proposal SOUND: access ergonomics for electric mobility in cross-border territories

Doctoral application at UTC AVENUES lab · science mediation as a methodological component

The project

In European cross-border territories marked by institutional and energy discontinuities, does the electric mobility transition narrow or widen inequalities of access?

Cross-border spaces are not simple “transit zones”: they are made of dense daily commuting flows, cut by different regulations, tariffs, standards and energy systems on either side. Around areas with strong economic and land-price differentials such as Luxembourg, this cut is especially visible — living on one side and working on the other directly amplifies travel constraints and structural vulnerability.

The core concept is access ergonomics (ergonomie d’accès): it accounts not only for distance and time but also for organisational, economic and cognitive constraints. Applied to electric mobility, it means going beyond the “are there enough charging stations” view to look at comprehension costs, technical management costs and range anxiety.

Research question

Under what conditions does the electric mobility transition reduce inequalities of access to mobility, and in which situations does it risk reinforcing them — particularly in cross-border territories marked by institutional and energy discontinuities?

Three hypotheses

  1. The border is an amplifier: the discontinuities specific to cross-border spaces amplify inequalities in access ergonomics, which result from a combination of spatial, economic, energy and informational factors
  2. PV and smart charging have distributive effects: integrating charging infrastructure into smart energy systems (photovoltaics, smart charging) improves environmental performance but produces differentiated distributive effects across territories and user profiles
  3. Coordinated policy is the key lever: policies coordinated at the cross-border level are crucial to reducing these inequalities

Methods

  • Spatial analysis: GIS and multi-criteria methods comparing theoretical accessibility with actual conditions of use
  • Qualitative surveys: interviews with cross-border workers to understand charging practices, adaptation strategies and perceived constraints
  • Indicators and typology: an access-ergonomics index (spatial, economic, energy, informational dimensions) feeding a typology of cross-border populations
  • Multi-agent modelling: simulating policy scenarios — business as usual, stronger cross-border coordination, smart charging development

Science mediation: 32 days a year

This is not “some outreach on the side” but workload written into the methodology:

  • Exploratory phase: identify key actors and surface variables absent from databases — the cognitive load of trip planning, informational obstacles, interoperability problems, the gap between theoretical accessibility and actual appropriation
  • Indicator phase: participatory workshops, user tests and intermediate outputs (maps, visualisations) to test the relevance, legibility and appropriability of indicators
  • Validation phase: intermediate validation of modelling results
  • Transfer phase: cross-border seminars, digital atlas and teaching materials

Deliverables

Doctoral manuscript; at least one mid-term journal article (typology of cross-border populations and access inequalities); follow-up articles (modelling and scenarios); a digital atlas and teaching materials; a cross-border seminar.

Fieldwork

A comparative sweep of several French borders first, then a deeper focus on the Franco-German-Swiss border.
