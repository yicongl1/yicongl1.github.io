Football robots

RoboCom · China Robot and AI Competition

ResultNational 2nd prize · National 3rd prize Period2022 My roleStrategy and control

The competition

iLoboke 3v3 robot football: three small robots play against each other inside a fenced field. They have to locate the ball, teammates and opponents using their own vision and communication, then pass, dribble and shoot — no remote control, no human time-outs.

In 2022 we entered two competition circuits: the China Robot and AI Competition (CRAIC) and the RoboCom Developers Competition. We won first prize in the Shanghai region and second prize in the national final at CRAIC, and first prize at the provincial final and third prize at the national final at RoboCom.

My role

  • Implemented low-level motion and decision interfaces in C++, with tactical scripts written in Lua;
  • Worked on passing, shooting and autonomous movement: when to release the ball, where to run, and how to keep three robots from crowding the same spot;
  • Tuned the tactics in simulation first, then on the real field. A perfect pass in simulation becomes something else entirely once friction, latency and localisation error are involved.

Takeaways

Simulation and reality are separated by uncertainty. In simulation the robot knows the exact position of the ball; on the real field it only has a noisy estimate. A strategy cannot assume it knows — it has to assume it might be wrong and leave room to correct.

Tactics are a team sport, and so is the code. Three robots share one field, so any robot’s decision changes what is optimal for the other two. In the end we stopped writing “what each robot should do” and wrote “what to do given what you see”, letting behaviour emerge from local interactions.

Debugging time is never enough. The window for code changes before a match is short, so parameters had to be adjustable from the sideline and logs had to be replayable. Making failures visible was worth more than writing a smarter strategy.
