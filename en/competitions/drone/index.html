Smart delivery drone

Shanghai University Students' Engineering Practice and Innovation Competition

ResultGrand Prize (Shanghai) · 1st prize (university) Period2022 My roleFlight-attitude simulation and tuning

The competition

The “Smart Delivery Drone” event asks a drone to complete a full mission autonomously: fly through obstacles, identify and grasp a target, then release the payload at a designated spot. It is not only about getting airborne — it is about completing a chain of actions reliably without hitting anything or losing the target.

In 2022 our four-person team won first prize at the university qualifier, then represented the university at the 12th Shanghai University Students’ Engineering Practice and Innovation Competition and received the Grand Prize in this event.

My role

  • Responsible for flight-attitude simulation and tuning: validating control parameters in simulation, then transferring them to the real aircraft;
  • Worked with the vision and control teammates to split “obstacle crossing — recognition — grasping — release” into stages that could be debugged independently;
  • The team used Linux, OpenCV, 3D modelling and 3D printing for the control stack and the structural parts of the payload mechanism.

Takeaways

Make it stable before making it smart. If the attitude is unstable, every downstream vision and grasping problem is amplified into vibration. Time spent tuning is not wasted — it decides whether the mission code ever gets a chance to finish.

Divided work is not separate work. Flight control, vision and structure are tightly coupled: a failed grasp may not be a vision problem at all, but a drifting attitude. A short daily alignment on interfaces and failure symptoms was more effective than everyone debugging in isolation.

Leave a way out for failure. At a competition you usually get one run. Being able to degrade, retry or take over manually is more practical than insisting on full autonomy.
