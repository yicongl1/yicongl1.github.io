Punch-list tracking and assisted localisation on site

Cœur Défense office floors, Paris La Défense · ART X BAT · TN10 final internship

PeriodSep 2025 - Feb 2026 My roleEngineering coordination intern (MOEX / OPC) InstitutionART X BAT

Repository ↗ Company showcase ↗ Academic record →

Context

This was my TN10 final internship at ART X BAT, working in MOEX / OPC — execution-phase client representation, scheduling, coordination and site supervision.

I followed three very different operations: a new build, an urban renovation, and the refurbishment of office floors in a high-rise (IGH) building at Paris La Défense (floors 34–39, Tours A/B). The high-rise became the main case study.

The site constraints were concrete:

  • strict access, visitor and authorisation control;
  • limited delivery dock, delivery slots and storage;
  • heavy trade co-activity and many interfaces;
  • highly repetitive office layouts with regular ceiling grids and technical equipment;
  • a tight schedule and a surge of punch-list items during OPR (pre-handover operations).

In this context, MOEX/OPC is not about “recording progress”: it is about turning site information into actionable work — locate, describe, assign, follow up, verify.

What I did

Site tracking

  • Attended meetings, took notes and clarified technical points;
  • checked headcount by trade against the planned progress;
  • carried out site inspections, photos and measurements;
  • tracked badges, access rights and technical-area authorisation;
  • coordinated the delivery dock, transport slots, storage, waste and site traffic;
  • updated progress tables by floor and zone;
  • wrote clarification, reminder and traceability emails.

Quality and punch list

  • Identified and consolidated punch-list items by trade;
  • structured them in IDCapture: location, package, contractor, description, deadline, photo;
  • maintained statuses: open, contractor-closed, MOE-validated, disputed, rejected;
  • used filters and statistics to prepare follow-up lists;
  • joined OPR inspections and published consolidated lists;
  • set up a dispute form to create a traceable client/contractor process.

Tool rollout

  • mapped participants and subcontractors into an account list;
  • checked users, roles and permissions;
  • prepared cleaned, zone-split PDF drawings;
  • designed a three-level contractor / MOE / client validation workflow;
  • configured the “site tracking / OPR / handover” sessions;
  • coordinated with the software vendor;
  • introduced the tool to contractors, shared tutorials and organised training.

The problem: a punch-list item without a location fails

A useful item answers five questions:

  1. Where: floor, zone, drawing, reference point;
  2. What: a sufficiently technical description;
  3. Who: the responsible package and contractor;
  4. When: deadline or target date;
  5. Evidence: photos, status history, acceptance record.

During OPR the number of items rises quickly. In repetitive office spaces, the same defect can appear dozens of times. To reduce input, teams often create a “general item” (réserve générale) grouping several occurrences into one record.

On paper this is efficient. On site, the record loses its precise location: a contractor may fix some occurrences and miss others, while the MOE cannot check how many points existed or whether all of them are closed.

The result: input becomes easier, but verification, reminders and communication become harder; the risk of omissions, duplicates and untreated items rises.

OpenCV prototype

The goal was not to replace people, but to test whether a site photo can produce usable location information. I built two pipelines.

Case A — missing sprinkler rosette

The test image contains a manual red circle:

  1. convert BGR to HSV;
  2. isolate the two red hue ranges around 0° and 180°;
  3. merge the masks;
  4. apply closing and opening;
  5. extract external contours and filter small areas;
  6. convert the main contour into a bounding box.

The chain is stable when the visual signal is strong, but it detects the annotation, not the missing rosette.

Case B — open ceiling plenum

This case is closer to reality and does not rely on annotation:

  1. grayscale and Gaussian blur;
  2. blackhat with a wide rectangular kernel to enhance dark joints;
  3. Canny edges;
  4. HoughLinesP for horizontal and vertical segments;
  5. merge nearby positions and rebuild the ceiling grid;
  6. split into cells and compute mean, standard deviation and Laplacian texture energy;
  7. select “abnormal” cells;
  8. inside those cells, use blackhat, Otsu thresholding, morphology and area/aspect-ratio/local-contrast filters to find elongated dark openings.

The pipeline produces candidate regions, but one opening may produce several boxes. Site visuals vary too much for fixed rules; a punch-list item is a contractual and engineering concept, not just a dark area.

Results and limits

  • Assisted localisation is useful: a photo can become a mask, contour, candidate box or grid cell — the first step towards pre-filling an item.
  • A single detection is not enough: a representative annotated dataset, a defect taxonomy by package, an independent validation set and acceptance metrics are required.
  • The reasonable automation is assistance: propose a zone, pre-fill a record, then let the MOE confirm.
  • Supervised learning was not trained during the internship because of time and missing annotations; the report frames it as a perspective, not a delivered result.
